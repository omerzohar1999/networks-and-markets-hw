\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The rapid spread of epidemics in networks, such as infectious diseases in human populations or computer viruses in digital networks, poses significant threats \cite{Cite1,Cite2}. Controlling such epidemics requires effective immunization strategies that can minimize the spread by vaccinating a subset of nodes \cite{Cite3,Cite4}, for instance in the case of short supply of vaccines or limited resources, such as at the beginning of a pandemic---as we have seen with COVID-19. Traditional immunization approaches often focus on preemptive vaccination without considering the current state of the epidemic, which may not be optimal once an outbreak has begun.

In this work, we address the \textit{Data-Aware Vaccination (DAV)} problem, which involves selecting the best set of nodes to vaccinate, given prior information about the infection's spread \cite{Cite5,Cite6}. Without knowledge of existing DAV algorithms, we independently hypothesized that \textbf{PageRank centrality} could serve as an effective heuristic for identifying critical nodes for vaccination. PageRank is a measure of node importance based on the structure of the network and has been widely used in ranking web pages \cite{page1999pagerank}.

We compare the performance of various vaccination strategies, including Random, Degree-based, PageRank-based, and NetShield algorithms \cite{tong2010vulnerability}, under the Independent Cascade (IC) and Susceptible-Infected-Recovered (SIR) models \cite{kempe2003maximizing,kermack1927contribution}. Our experiments on real-world networks demonstrate that the PageRank-based strategy significantly outperforms other heuristics in minimizing the epidemic's spread.

\textbf{Contributions}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose using PageRank centrality as a heuristic for node vaccination in the DAV problem.
    \item We implement and evaluate the effectiveness of various vaccination strategies on real-world networks.
    \item We demonstrate that the PageRank-based strategy significantly reduces the spread of epidemics compared to other heuristics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Vaccination strategies in networks have been extensively studied \cite{Cite11,Cite12}. Preemptive immunization methods often rely on network centrality measures, such as degree or betweenness, to select nodes for vaccination \cite{Cite13}. Data-aware approaches consider the current state of the epidemic to make vaccination decisions \cite{Cite14,Cite15}.

The NetShield algorithm \cite{tong2010vulnerability} aims to select nodes that minimize the spectral radius of the adjacency matrix, thereby reducing the network's vulnerability to epidemic spread. Our work differs in that we explore the use of PageRank centrality as a heuristic for data-aware vaccination without prior knowledge of such algorithms.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be an undirected graph representing the network, where $V$ is the set of vertices and $E$ is the set of edges. Let $S \subseteq V$ represent the set of initially infected nodes. The objective is to select a subset $T \subseteq V \setminus S$ of $k$ healthy nodes to vaccinate, minimizing the spread of the epidemic.

This allows us to reason both about the spread of diseases in human populations, where nodes represent individuals, and about the spread of computer viruses in digital networks, where nodes represent computers or servers.

\subsection{Epidemic Models}\label{sec:epidemic-models}

We consider two epidemic models, each suited to different disease dynamics:

\paragraph{Susceptible-Infected-Recovered (SIR) Model \cite{kermack1927contribution}}

The SIR model is commonly used to study disease spread in networks \cite{anderson1991infectious, hethcote2000mathematics, shah2011rumors}. It models infectious diseases with varying recovery rates, and we thus use it to model the low-recovery rate disease like Ebola, known for its high mortality. Specifically, infected nodes can transmit the disease to susceptible neighbors with a high probability $p = 0.8$, and recover with a low probability $\delta = 0.1$, becoming immune and ceasing transmission.

\paragraph{Independent Cascade (IC) Model \cite{kempe2003maximizing}}

A variant of the SIR model, the IC model is used to simulate information diffusion in networks \cite{kempe2003maximizing}---by allowing nodes to infect neighbors only once. We use it to models diseases like COVID-19, where quick quarantine and recovery are common. Specifically, infected nodes infect susceptible neighbors with probability $p = 0.4$, but can only attempt infection once before becoming inactive.

\subsection{PageRank Centrality}

PageRank is a measure of node importance based on the idea that connections from important nodes contribute more to the importance of a node \cite{page1999pagerank}. The PageRank score $PR(i)$ of node $i$ is defined recursively as:

\begin{equation*}
PR(v) = \frac{\epsilon}{N} + (1 - \epsilon) \sum_{(v', v) \in E} \frac{PR(v')}{\text{out-degree}(v')}
\end{equation*}

\noindent
where $\epsilon$ is the damping factor (typically set to $1/7$), and $N$ is the total number of nodes.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

The \textbf{Data-Aware Vaccination (DAV)} problem involves selecting a set $S^* \subset V \setminus S$ of $k$ healthy nodes to vaccinate to minimize the expected number of infected nodes $\sigma_{G,S}(S)$ at the end of the epidemic:

\begin{equation*}
S^* = \arg \min_{S \subset V \setminus S, |S| = k} \sigma_{G,S}(S).
\end{equation*}

This problem is known to be NP-hard \cite{Cite16}, and exact solutions are computationally infeasible for large networks. Therefore, heuristic approaches are necessary.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Vaccination Strategies}

We implement and compare the following strategies:

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{|l|p{11cm}|}
        \hline
        \textbf{Strategy}     & \textbf{Description} \\ \hline
        \textbf{No Vaccination} & No nodes are vaccinated. \\ \hline
        \textbf{Random}        & Randomly select $k$ healthy nodes to vaccinate. \\ \hline
        \textbf{NetShield}     & Use the NetShield algorithm \cite{tong2010vulnerability} to select nodes that minimize the spectral radius. \\ \hline
        \textbf{Degree-based}  & Select the $k$ healthy nodes with the highest degrees. A degenerate case of PageRank, as discussed in class. \\ \hline
        \textbf{PageRank-based} & Select the $k$ healthy nodes with the highest PageRank scores. \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Evaluation Metric}
We evaluate each strategy based on the \textbf{average number of healthy nodes} remaining after the epidemic. A higher number of healthy nodes indicates a more effective vaccination strategy. This is averaged across the amount of simulations per configuration.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experimental_results}

Due to space limitations, we present a summary of our experimental results here. Detailed figures for each network under both epidemic models are provided in the \hyperref[sec:artifacts]{Artifacts Appendix} (see Figures~\ref{fig:ic_results} and \ref{fig:sir_results}).

Our experiments consistently demonstrate that the PageRank-based vaccination strategy significantly reduces the spread of epidemics compared to other heuristics across different networks and epidemic models. Moreover, we note a significant runtime improvement compared to the NetShield algorithm, which requires solving an optimization problem to minimize the spectral radius.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The effectiveness of the PageRank-based strategy can be attributed to its ability to identify nodes that are not only highly connected but also central in the network's structure. These nodes have a higher potential to influence a large portion of the network \cite{Cite21}.

Our findings align with the principles of data-aware vaccination, which emphasizes the importance of utilizing network information and the current state of the epidemic in vaccination decisions \cite{Cite5}. While NetShield is designed to minimize the spectral radius, our results suggest that PageRank centrality offers a more practical and computationally efficient heuristic.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have shown that using PageRank centrality as a heuristic for node vaccination in the Data-Aware Vaccination problem is highly effective in controlling the spread of epidemics in networks. Our extensive experiments on real-world networks and epidemic models validate our hypothesis and demonstrate that the PageRank-based strategy outperforms other heuristics.

Future work includes exploring dynamic vaccination strategies that adapt to the evolving state of the epidemic and investigating the combination of PageRank with other centrality measures to further enhance vaccination effectiveness.
